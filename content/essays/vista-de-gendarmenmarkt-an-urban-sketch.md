Vista de Gendarmenmarkt en Berlin was made during Dr. Rizal’s sojourn in Germany’s capital. The drawing captures a view of a public square where the Schauspielhaus (Concert Hall), the Französischer Dom (French Cathedral), and the Deutscher Dom (German Cathedral) are located.

Rizal stayed in Germany from 1886 to 1887. The reason was to specialize in ophthalmology and cure his mother’s eyesight. He also had the option to study in England, but chose Germany for its lower cost of living, and because few Filipinos knew German. He studied Germany from afar before he set foot in it. News about Germany’s economic prosperity would reach him from Paris and Madrid. He also learned about Germany’s cultural advancement from the works of Schiller, Heine, and Lessing. His interest in the German language was evident from his purchase of German books and grammar lessons. But what likely attracted Rizal most to Germany was its reputation as the seat of science, technology, and medicine at that time.

Rizal arrived in Germany in 1886, and lived in Wilhelmsfeld for three months, which is called “Noli Village” today, while the street beside the parsonage or house of the local pastor is named “Jose Rizal Strasse”. Rizal discovered the work of Professor Ferdinand Blumentritt, Vocabulario Tagalog-Aleman, and wrote him a letter saying he was “astonished that you have taken so much trouble in studying a language like Tagalog which is so difficult”.

On 1 November 1886, Rizal traveled to Berlin, the political and cultural center of Europe in the 1880s, where he became acquainted with prominent people in the scientific and literary community, including Dr. Fedor Jagor and Dr. Rudolf Virchow. Scholars recommended the membership of Dr. Rizal in the prestigious Anthropological Society, the Ethnological Society and the Geographic Society, making Rizal the first Asian scientist to be admitted to full membership in the three leading European scientific societies.

Rizal again wrote to Blumentritt on 12 January 1887, praising German and English scholars “for letting a little light penetrate our dark country.” He writes, “It is marvelous that these strangers, without having visited our country, do not judge us according to our deep-rooted prejudices, as others do, but rather with a liberal criterion and in a humanitarian sense. We are all human and we can improve ourselves through education and culture, as other peoples did which only some centuries ago were still savages.”

One of the last letters Rizal wrote to Blumentritt from Berlin was dated 24 April 1887 where he refers to Berlin as “my scientific mother country.” On 6 June 1887, he wrote to Blumentritt from Geneva: “When I hear German spoken I am glad, as if it were my mother tongue.”

Rizal left Berlin on 11 May 1887 to tour Germany with his friend Maximo Viola, who sponsored the printing of Noli Me Tangere.

Vista de Gendarmenmarkt en Berlin was acquired by the National Museum of the Philippines through the generous gift of Aurora Ortega-Carlos in memory of her spouse, Pablo C. Carlos.
