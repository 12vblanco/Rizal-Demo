Rizal chose the pen rather than the paint brush or the sculptor’s chisel. Yet, his travel notes and letters to home had sketches of people, landscapes, whatever he considered memorable. He illustrated the Tagalog translation of Hans Christien Andersen’s fairy tales he made for his nephews and nieces. He drew a self-portrait and sent it to Ferdinand Blumentritt ; there are two extant portraits of his sisters Saturnina and Lucia. He drew his nipa house in Dapitan and the oracle, Sybilla Cumaná.

Rizal dabbled in sculpture using wood, clay, tuff and bakawan (mangrove) paste. He made statuettes of saints and local people, a lion’s head for a potable water spout. Two of his pieces allude to colonial oppression:” Mother’s Revenge”, a dog grappling with a crocodile devouring its pup and “ Triumph of Death Over Life “ and the path to liberation is “ Triumph of science over Death“. “Prometheus Bound” is Rizal’s own story; Prometheus stole fire from Mt. Olympus to benefit mankind which angered the gods; Zeus bound him to a rock so an eagle could eat his liver daily.

For the multi-faceted man that was Rizal, sentimiento nacional was the defining characteristic throughout his vast contributions in diverse fields, and his eye for art was no exception. In this digital exhibition, Rizal’s art serves as anecdotes of his experiences, allegories, and ideas. Be it in his literary magnus opus or miniature sculptures, the artist in Rizal reveals a window into his complex mind.
